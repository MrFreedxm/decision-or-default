# Example: a portfolio hero, three rounds

The client asked for a first screen for a personal portfolio. What happened when the critic was run before each show.

**Round 1 - eight "loud" variants.** A flashlight over giant type, a rotating 3D prism of five screens, a terminal that types itself, a giant nickname with a phone passing between the letters, a row of lit windows, blocks flying into a grid, a chat, a desk seen from above.
Pass 1 said: the category default for "developer portfolio hero" is exactly "big type + one animated object". Six of eight were that default in different clothes. Client: "big 3D objects - nonsense, not for a portfolio; loud letters and words - no."

**Round 2 - four quiet variants.** One sentence on the left; two columns; centered like a business card; a sentence at the bottom edge of the screen. No animation.
Pass 1-bis, question 1: the client chose by number in one message ("3, more or less"). Question 2 came from the client, not the critic: the headline listed services ("requests, CRM, documents"), which narrows what the person does; rewritten as one broad sentence the client supplied.

**Round 3 - three variants off references.** A reference sheet from a catalogue of live sites (a photo-plus-sentence layout, a changelog-like "building now" list, a question-and-answer pair). Client kept the centered one from round 2 and rejected "building now" outright: "nonsense, never mention it anywhere".

**What the taste file got from this:**
- Named good: centered, one sentence, three text links, a thin rule and three facts under it. Principle: the quietest construction that still has one axis.
- Rejected: big objects in a hero; demos on the front screen; a list of services in the headline; "what I am building now".
- Method that worked: numbered variants on one stable address, switched with keys; the client answered in numbers.

Verdict of the kept variant: PASS, 7/10 - "does its job quietly, no wow move, and none was asked for".
